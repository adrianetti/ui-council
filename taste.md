## Basics that hold in every style

- Readable: body text at a comfortable size, contrast that passes in every theme, lines short enough to read.
- One clear focal point on each screen. Secondary things recede.
- A deliberate system: a small type scale, a limited palette, spacing and corner radii decided once and reused.
- Alignment to a grid. Related things sit close, unrelated things sit apart.
- Interactive things look interactive. The main action is obvious. Hover, focus, selected and disabled states are clear.
- Real, specific content: the product's own words, objects and data, not filler.
- Consistency: the same thing looks and behaves the same everywhere.

## Fidelity to the design language

- Judge the execution within the product's own design language. A pixel-art site should be excellent pixel art. A kindergarten site should be excellent playful design. A futuristic site should be an excellent futuristic site. Never pull a design toward a neutral "clean" template.
- Choices the design language calls for are not flaws, for example bright primaries for a kindergarten, pixel type and hard shadows for retro, or neon glows for a futuristic look.
- Breaking the language is a flaw, for example a generic component dropped into a styled page, a default font on a page with its own type, or a corporate palette in a playful brand.
- Distinctive beats safe. A screen that could belong to any product scores low on taste, however tidy it is.

## Generic AI-template tells

These count against a screen unless the design language deliberately calls for them. One tell alone costs little. Several together make a screen read as a template.

Color and surface:

- Indigo or violet as the accent (the "assistant palette"), or a cream ground with terracotta.
- Gradient backgrounds, and gradients on buttons or text. Glows. Decorative gradient blobs.
- Many shades and opacities of one color doing the work of a real palette.
- Drop shadows on things at rest, such as cards and panels.
- Glass panels (backdrop blur). Dithering or ASCII art as decoration.

Type:

- Instrument Serif, or another trendy display serif, with italic accent words in the headline.
- Italics for emphasis all over the page.
- Tight negative letter-spacing on headings.
- Monospace for text that is not code.
- Headings and taglines written as clipped sentences that end in periods, such as "Plans first. Acts second."
- ALL CAPS subtitles. Tiny uppercase letter-spaced labels.

Structure:

- Eyebrows: small labels or pill badges above headings. Section numbers such as "01 ·" or "02 / PLAN".
- Tags and pills everywhere. Squircles. Floating pill navigation.
- Every block in its own bordered container. Dividers between everything.
- Code-style breadcrumbs or file paths as decoration.
- Over-detailed UI mockups and diagrams that show more than anyone can read.
- Icons in tinted rounded squares or circles. KPI tiles with an icon, a green "+12.5%" and a sparkline.
- Rows of identical cards. A "Welcome back" header with a waving hand.
- Emoji or sparkles as decoration.

Words:

- Verbose, redundant copy that says the same thing twice.

The whole:

- A stock kit look: default font, near-white ground, white cards with a 12px radius. Swap the product name and nothing would change.

Removing a tell earns nothing by itself. A screen with no tells and no clear hierarchy is still weak.

## App screens and pages

- App screens, which people work in (dashboards, settings, lists): color goes on data, not chrome. Surfaces stay flat. Headings stay modest. Navigation recedes. Tables are alignment and quiet rules.
- Pages, which people read and scroll (sites, landing pages): one dominant idea or image per screen. Gaps between sections are clearly larger than gaps inside them. One strong headline of two lines or fewer.
